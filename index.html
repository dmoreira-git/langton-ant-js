FIELD NOTES · 001

An ant with
four moves.

Langton's ant is a two-rule universe. It walks a square grid, reads the color of the cell beneath it, turns, flips that cell, and moves forward. That is the whole law.

  1. 01
    Read the tile. Each state maps to a turn: R, L, N, or U.
  2. 02
    Flip it. The tile advances to its next state and glows in the palette.
  3. 03
    Move. One cell forward. Repeat. No randomness, ever.
THE ORIGINAL
0 → turn right, flip to 1 · 1 → turn left, flip to 0

Why does it loop?

For the classic rule, the ant wanders chaotically for roughly 10,000 steps. Then, without warning, it starts building a repeating 104-step “highway” that translates across the grid forever. The Loop Lab fingerprints a 21 × 21 patch around the ant. When an identical patch returns, it records the period and the drift, and draws the cycle.

Different rules find different loops — or none at all. Try a preset, randomize the rule, and watch how order emerges from a tiny change.
